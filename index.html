AstraSync AI/ML Contract Decision

Which pricing offer should AstraSync accept?  ·  Present value of contribution, 1.5% monthly discount rate, 18 months

Summary Analysis Illustration Sensitivity Analysis Conclusion

1Summary

AstraSync proposed $160,000 a month for an 18-month AI/ML implementation (PV contribution $626,902), but the client rejected it and offered two cheaper options, with an open RFP as the fallback. We valued each path as the present value of AstraSync's contribution after its $120,000 monthly cost, discounting at 1.5% per month. We used a decision tree for the bonus and bid outcomes and a 10,000-run triangular simulation of client savings to value the RFP gain-share. The RFP bid at $150,000/month has the highest expected value ($509,768), narrowly ahead of the bonus offer ($506,713) and the flat $152,000 offer ($501,522). Because the gap is under $9,000 and the RFP carries a 60% chance of earning nothing, we recommend AstraSync bid on the RFP only if it is confident its win chance is at least 40%; otherwise it should accept option (b), the $125,000 plus bonus offer.

2Analysis

AstraSync's cost is 1,200 hours × $100 = $120,000 per month. The 18-month annuity factor at 1.5% is 15.6726 and the month-18 discount factor is 0.7649.

Equal payments of $152,000/mo

$501,522

  • PV = $32,000 × 15.6726.
  • No risk: the payoff does not depend on performance or competition.
  • Gives up $125,380 against the original proposal.
Option B

$125,000/mo + $800,000 bonus

$506,713
  • Monthly contribution is only $5,000 (PV $78,363).
  • The $800,000 bonus is paid at month 18 if the implementation beats the client's benchmark (PV $611,929).
  • With bonus: $690,292 (p = 0.7). Without: $78,363 (p = 0.3).
  • Moderate risk: 30% chance of a very thin margin.
Option C ★

RFP bid at $150,000/mo + gain-share

$509,768
  • Monthly contribution of $30,000 (PV $470,177) if AstraSync wins.
  • Expected gain-share of $1,051,420 at month 18 (PV $804,243), from simulated savings.
  • If won: $1,274,420. If lost (p = 0.6): $0.
  • High risk: highest EV, but most likely outcome is nothing.

How the RFP gain-share was valued

Client savings follow a triangular distribution (low $3.2M, most likely $5.6M, high $12.8M). The tiered share was applied to each of 10,000 simulated savings values and averaged; an exact integration gives the same result.

4Sensitivity Analysis

The two most important inputs are the two subjective probabilities: AstraSync's chance of winning the RFP and its chance of earning the bonus. Both are estimates, and small changes in either one flip the recommendation.

What the sensitivity shows

  • The RFP's EV moves $12,744 for every 1-point change in win probability. Dropping from 40% to 39% makes (b) the better choice.
  • The bonus option's EV moves $6,119 for every 1-point change in bonus probability. Raising it from 70% to 71% makes (b) beat the RFP.
  • Option (a) is never far behind. It is the best choice only when both probabilities fall a little below their base values.
  • The base case sits almost exactly on the boundary between (b) and (c), so the recommendation is fragile.

5Conclusion

On expected value alone, AstraSync should decline both client offers and bid $150,000 a month in the RFP (EV $509,768). It edges out the $125,000-plus-bonus offer ($506,713) and the flat $152,000 offer ($501,522). None of the options comes close to the original $160,000 proposal ($626,902), so AstraSync is giving up roughly $117,000 to $125,000 in value whichever path it takes.

The margin is very thin, though. The RFP pays nothing 60% of the time, and the recommendation reverses if the win probability is even one point lower than 40%, or the bonus probability one point higher than 70%. Those probabilities are judgment calls, not measured data.

Recommendation: AstraSync's long track record makes it confident in delivery, which favors option (b): the bonus rewards exactly what AstraSync controls, while the RFP depends on competitors it cannot control. Unless the director has strong evidence that AstraSync's chance of winning the RFP is above 40%, the risk-adjusted choice is to accept option (b) and push the client for a slightly higher monthly base payment. A risk-neutral firm with confidence in its bid could reasonably choose the RFP.
